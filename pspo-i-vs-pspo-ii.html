PSPO I vs PSPO II: which Product Owner certification should you take first?

PSPO I proves you know what a Product Owner is accountable for. PSPO II proves you can actually maximise value when stakeholders, budgets and roadmaps pull in different directions. Here's how to choose.

By ExamFastTrack Updated 2026 8 min read

Scrum.org's Professional Scrum Product Owner track has two main levels, and just like the Scrum Master track, people often assume level II is "level I but harder." It isn't — it tests a different skill.

The one-line difference

PSPO I checks whether you know the Product Owner accountabilities. PSPO II checks whether you can make good product decisions under real-world pressure.

PSPO I: do you know the role?

  • Format: 80 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes.
  • Pass mark: 85%.
  • Prerequisite: none.
  • Feels like: a fast knowledge test on the Scrum Guide from the Product Owner's point of view — Product Backlog ownership, the Product Goal, Sprint Review, and who decides what.

The classic trap: answers that describe how Product Owners work in many companies ("the PO assigns tasks", "the PO approves the Sprint Backlog") rather than what the Scrum Guide says. The exam punishes those every time.

If you can't explain from memory who owns the Product Backlog, who owns the Sprint Backlog, and who can cancel a Sprint, you're not ready for PSPO I yet.

PSPO II: can you maximise value?

  • Format: 40 questions in 60 minutes.
  • Pass mark: 85%.
  • Prerequisite: none officially, but PSPO I knowledge is assumed.
  • Feels like: a product-management judgement test — vision, value, stakeholder management, product strategy, forecasting and evidence-based decisions.

Several answers will sound reasonable. The right one is the option that best serves value and empiricism — not the one that keeps the most stakeholders happy in the short term.

So which one first?

For almost everyone: PSPO I first, then PSPO II. PSPO I builds the vocabulary that PSPO II scenarios take for granted, and it's the credential most job listings actually ask for. PSPO II is the differentiator once you have it.

If you're a Scrum Master rather than a Product Owner, see our PSM I vs PSM II guide instead — or take PSPO I as a complement, since both share the same Scrum Guide foundation.

Where PAL I fits

PAL I (Professional Agile Leadership) is aimed at managers, executives and leaders who support agile teams — not at the Product Owner role itself. It's a great add-on if you lead people or departments, but it isn't a stepping stone between PSPO I and PSPO II. Try the free PAL I questions to see the style.

Common questions

Can I take PSPO II without PSPO I?

Yes — there's no formal prerequisite. But PSPO II assumes PSPO I knowledge, so most people pass PSPO I first.

What's the passing score?

85% for both. PSPO I: 80 questions in 60 minutes. PSPO II: 40 questions in 60 minutes.

Is PSPO II much harder?

Yes. It moves from knowing the role to making product decisions — value, strategy, stakeholders — where several answers sound plausible.

Do Scrum.org certifications expire?

No. They don't expire and have no renewal fees.
